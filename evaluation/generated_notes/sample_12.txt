**Chief Complaint:** Left shoulder pain.

**Key Symptoms:**
The patient reports a constant left shoulder pain that worsens with movement, particularly when reaching or lifting objects, and is unbearable when lying on the affected shoulder. The pain has been present for approximately three weeks.
- **Pertinent Negatives:** Denies numbness, tingling, fever, or chest pain.

**Relevant Medical History:**
- The patient is active and has been renovating his basement, which may have contributed to the injury.
- No previous significant shoulder problems, but occasional aches and pains relieved by Tylenol.

**Physical Examination & Vitals:**
- Vitals: All vitals are within normal limits.
- Shoulder: Limited active and passive range of motion, tenderness of the greater tuberosity of the humerus, but no tenderness at the sternoclavicular or acromioclavicular joints.
- Neurovascular exam: Good hand grips, capillary refill less than three seconds, and sensation intact to light touch.

**Assessment & Rationale:**
1. Rotator cuff tendinopathy: Suggested by the patient's symptoms, limited range of motion, and tenderness of the greater tuberosity of the humerus.

**Treatment Plan & Next Steps:**
- Diagnostics: An MRI of the left shoulder has been ordered to confirm the diagnosis and rule out other potential causes.
- Medication: The patient may continue to take Tylenol for pain management.
- Therapy: The patient will be referred to physical therapy for approximately six to eight weeks to strengthen the shoulder muscles.
- Follow-up: A follow-up visit is scheduled after completing physical therapy to assess the patient's progress. If symptoms do not improve, a steroid injection may be considered.
