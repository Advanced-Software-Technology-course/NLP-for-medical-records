**Chief Complaint:** Philip, presents with right knee pain.

**History of Present Illness:**
The patient reports slipping and falling on his property, which occurred a week prior to the visit. He describes the pain as being exacerbated when flexing his knee and applying pressure. He heard a "pop" during the incident and has been experiencing some swelling. The patient reports that when stationary, he does not really feel a lot of pain. The pain is rated 4/10 when mobile, but is significantly less when stationary. He has been taking ibuprofen for swelling.

**Relevant Medical History:**
- Conditions: Hypertension managed by 20 milligrams of lisinopril.
- Regional Trauma: No explicit denial or confirmation of prior injury to the right knee.

**Physical Examination & Vitals:**
- Vitals: BP: 150/70. The doctor notes that the patient might have a little white coat syndrome.
- Systemic Clearances: 
  - The patient denies experiencing any chest pain.
  - The patient denies experiencing any belly pain.
  - The patient denies experiencing any shortness of breath.
  - Lungs are clear bilaterally, no wheezes or crackles; heart exam reveals a grade 2 out of 6 systolic ejection murmur, which was pre-existing.

**Localized Exam:** 
  - The patient experiences pain to palpation on the medial aspect of the right knee.
  - There is pain with flexion and extension of the knee.
  - Edema and effusion (fluid in the knee) are present.

**Assessment & Rationale:**
1. **Medial Collateral Ligament (MCL) Strain**: Supported by the clinical presentation of pain with flexion and extension, edema, effusion, and the mechanism of injury (twisting during the fall), and the absence of fractures or bony abnormalities on the X-ray.

**Treatment Plan & Next Steps:**
- Medications: Ibuprofen 800 milligrams, twice a day, to help with swelling and pain.
- Immobilization: A knee brace to support the muscles and allow healing.
- Therapy: Ice the knee for 20 minutes, 3 to 4 times a day, to reduce swelling.
- Logistics & Follow-up: 
  - The patient is advised to elevate his leg, stay off it, and consider having others assist with physical tasks for a couple of weeks. 
  - The patient is advised to tell someone (likely a family member or caregiver) that it is the doctor's order for him to stay off his leg.
  - The doctor advises the patient to reduce his sodium intake to about 2300 milligrams per day. A referral to nutrition is planned to help manage his diet and hypertension. 
  - The patient will continue on 20 milligrams of lisinopril for hypertension. 
  - The nurse will be in with the orders, and follow-up details will be determined at a later time.
