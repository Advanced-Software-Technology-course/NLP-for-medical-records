**Chief Complaint:** Philip, presents with right knee pain.

**History of Present Illness:**
The patient reports an injury occurring 1 week prior to the consultation, when he slipped and fell while doing work on his property. The patient reported feeling a pop at the time of injury. Pain is rated 4-5/10 with mobility, but negligible when stationary, aggravated by flexion and extension of the knee, and associated symptoms include swelling and pain when pressure is applied to the knee.
- **Pertinent Negatives:** Denies chest pain, belly pain, and shortness of breath.

**Relevant Medical History:**
- Conditions: Hypertension. The patient is currently taking 20 milligrams of Lisinopril for hypertension. The patient has a goal to reduce sodium intake to 2300 milligrams per day.
- Regional Trauma: The patient does not mention any prior injury to the right knee.

**Physical Examination & Vitals:**
- Vitals: The patient's current blood pressure is 150/70.
- Systemic Clearances: Lungs are clear bilaterally, no wheezes or crackles. Heart exam reveals a grade 2 out of 6 systolic ejection murmur.
- Localized Exam (MSK): The patient has pain to palpation of the medial aspect of the right knee, pain with flexion and extension, edema, and effusion. Edema and effusion are present around the knee. The outside of the knee is not painful to palpation.

**Assessment & Rationale:**
1. **Medial Collateral Ligament (MCL) Strain**: Supported by the clinical presentation of pain with flexion and extension, edema, effusion, and pain to palpation of the medial aspect of the right knee, as well as the mechanism of injury. An X-ray of the right knee showed no fractures or bony abnormalities.

**Treatment Plan & Next Steps:**
- Medications: Ibuprofen, 800 milligrams, twice a day, for pain and swelling.
- Immobilization: A knee brace will be used to support the knee and allow it to heal.
- Therapy: The patient is instructed to ice the knee for 20 minutes, 3-4 times a day, to reduce swelling.
- Logistics & Follow-up: The patient is advised to elevate the leg and stay off it. The patient is advised to avoid yard work for a couple of weeks. A referral to nutrition is recommended to help manage his diet and hypertension. Follow-up with the doctor will be scheduled at a later time. The patient will receive orders from the nurse.
